# 9. Voice vs Tone

The skill must maintain this distinction:

```text
VOICE = stable verbal character
TONE = situational adjustment
```

Example:

The voice may always be:

- clear
- confident
- human

while tone becomes:

- calmer in support
- more energetic in launch communication
- more formal in legal or B2B communication

---

# 10. Messaging vs Copy

Messaging is the strategic system of what the brand must communicate.

Copy is a specific execution.

Phase 5 creates both:

- messaging rules
- representative examples

But the system should never confuse a single headline with the whole messaging strategy.

# STEP 0 — VERBAL READINESS & IDENTITY TRANSLATION

# 20. Objective

Convert the Phase 4 character system into explicit verbal requirements before writing any core messages.

---

# 21. Identity-to-Language Mapping

Translate:

```text
PERSONALITY TRAIT
↓
VERBAL BEHAVIOR
```

Example:

```yaml
trait:
  "Approachable expertise"

verbal_behavior:
  - explain technical ideas simply
  - never talk down to the customer
  - use evidence without sounding academic
  - avoid unnecessary jargon
```

---

# 22. Relationship-to-Language Mapping

Example:

If the brand role is `GUIDE`:

- language should help decisions
- give direction without domination
- reduce uncertainty
- explain trade-offs

If the brand role is `EXPERT`:

- language should show competence
- make claims carefully
- provide proof
- avoid casual unsupported opinions

---

# 23. Behavioral Principle Translation

Each behavioral principle should have verbal consequences.

Example:

```yaml
behavioral_principle:
  "Explain before persuading"

verbal_implications:
  - provide context before CTA
  - do not hide limitations
  - explain why a choice matters
  - avoid manipulation
```

---

# 24. Must Sound Like / Must Not Sound Like

Create early guardrails.

Example:

```text
MUST SOUND LIKE:
clear
useful
confident
human

MUST NOT SOUND LIKE:
hype-heavy
patronizing
generic
bureaucratic
```

---

# 25. Customer Language Intake

Import customer words and phrases from Phase 2.

Classify:

```text
PROBLEM_LANGUAGE
DESIRED_OUTCOME_LANGUAGE
DECISION_CRITERIA_LANGUAGE
OBJECTION_LANGUAGE
CATEGORY_LANGUAGE
EMOTIONAL_LANGUAGE
```

---

# 26. Customer Language Rule

Use customer language to improve resonance.

Do not blindly copy slang, misspellings, or inaccurate beliefs.

---

# 27. Category Language Audit

Identify common category phrases.

Classify them:

```text
NECESSARY
USEFUL
OVERUSED
GENERIC
MISLEADING
TECHNICAL
REGULATED
```

---

# 28. Cliché Detection

Examples of generic language:

- best quality
- innovative solutions
- customer-centric
- world-class
- premium experience
- unique solution

These may be used only if defined and proven.

# STEP 1 — BRAND VOICE SYSTEM

# 30. Objective

Define the stable verbal character of the brand.

---

# 31. Brand Voice Definition

Brand voice is:

> the consistent verbal personality that should remain recognizable across channels and situations.

---

# 32. Voice Principle Count

Prefer:

```text
3 to 5
```

core voice principles.

---

# 33. Voice Principle Structure

Each principle must include:

```yaml
principle:
meaning:
strategic_reason:
customer_effect:
how_it_sounds:
how_it_does_not_sound:
do:
dont:
before_after_example:
```

---

# 34. Voice Principle Example

```text
Clear before clever.
```

Meaning:

> The brand prioritizes understanding over wordplay.

Do:

> Choose the plan that fits your current team size.

Don't:

> Unlock a next-level journey into organizational excellence.

---

# 35. Voice Traits

Voice traits should derive from Phase 4 personality.

Examples:

```text
CLEAR
CONFIDENT
HUMAN
PRECISE
WARM
DIRECT
CURIOUS
CALM
ENERGETIC
PRAGMATIC
```

Use only strategically relevant traits.

---

# 36. Voice Trait Hierarchy

Separate:

```text
PRIMARY
SUPPORTING
PROHIBITED
```

---

# 37. Voice Trait Tension

Useful verbal tensions include:

```text
EXPERT BUT SIMPLE
CONFIDENT BUT NOT ARROGANT
WARM BUT NOT OVERFAMILIAR
DIRECT BUT NOT RUDE
PREMIUM BUT NOT PRETENTIOUS
PLAYFUL BUT NOT CHILDISH
```

---

# 38. Voice Consistency Rule

Voice should remain stable across:

- website
- social
- sales
- support
- product
- email
- advertising

while tone changes.

---

# 39. Voice Anti-Pattern

Do not define voice only through adjectives.

Each trait needs observable writing behavior.

---

# 40. Voice Rule Categories

Define rules for:

- clarity
- sentence length
- jargon
- confidence
- evidence
- emotional expression
- directness
- humor
- customer respect
- persuasion
- urgency

---

# 41. Voice Before/After Examples

For each core principle, include at least one:

```text
OFF-BRAND
ON-BRAND
```

example in Persian.

---

# 42. Voice Stress Test

Test voice in:

- happy announcement
- complaint
- technical explanation
- sales pitch
- policy statement

If it becomes unrecognizable, refine.

---

# 43. Step 1 Output

Create:

# `Brand Voice System`

Including:

- voice definition
- 3–5 principles
- primary traits
- supporting traits
- prohibited traits
- verbal tensions
- do/don't rules
- examples
- consistency guidance

Tone is:

> the situational expression of the stable brand voice.

---

# 49. Tone Card

```yaml
context:

```

---

# 50. Complaint Tone

In complaints:

- reduce promotional energy
- increase ownership
- increase clarity
- avoid humor where inappropriate
- do not become defensive

---

# 51. Crisis Tone

In crisis:

- accuracy before personality
- clarity before cleverness
- responsibility before promotion
- facts before speculation

---

# 52. Celebration Tone

Celebration may increase:

- energy
- warmth
- expressiveness

but should remain within brand character.

---

# 53. Sales Tone

Sales tone must match brand relationship role.

Avoid artificial urgency if inconsistent with strategy.

---

# 54. Education Tone

Educational content should balance:

```text
ACCURACY
+
ACCESSIBILITY
```

---

# 55. Tone Drift Rule

Tone flexibility must not create a different brand in each channel.

---

# STEP 3 — MESSAGING ARCHITECTURE & MESSAGE HIERARCHY

# 57. Objective

Define what the brand must communicate first, second, and third.

---

# 58. Messaging Architecture Definition

Messaging architecture is:

> the structured system that organizes the most important brand messages by priority, audience, and proof.

---

# 59. Message Hierarchy Principle

Not every important fact deserves equal prominence.

Use:

```text
CORE MESSAGE
↓
MESSAGE PILLARS
↓
SUPPORTING MESSAGES
↓
PROOF
↓
DETAIL
```

---

# 60. Core Message

The core message should answer:

> Why should the primary target care about this brand?

It should connect:

```text
CUSTOMER NEED
+
VALUE
+
DIFFERENCE
```

---

# 61. Message Pillars

Prefer:

```text
3 to 5
```

message pillars.

They should derive from:

- value proposition
- differentiation
- brand promise
- strategic pillars
- customer decision criteria

---

# 62. Message Pillar Structure

```yaml
pillar:
core_message:
customer_need:
benefit:
proof:
objection_addressed:
supporting_points:
```

---

# 63. Feature-to-Benefit Rule

Do not stop at features.

Use:

```text
FEATURE
↓
FUNCTIONAL BENEFIT
↓
CUSTOMER OUTCOME
↓
EMOTIONAL / ECONOMIC VALUE
```

where appropriate.

---

# 64. Message Priority Rule

Priority should reflect:

- customer importance
- strategic importance
- differentiation
- proof strength
- purchase-stage relevance

---

# 65. Message Saturation Rule

Do not communicate every pillar in every piece of content.

Select the message appropriate to the context.

---

# 66. Audience Variant Rule

If multiple audiences exist:

- keep the brand core stable
- adapt emphasis
- do not create contradictory brand promises

---

# 67. Audience Message Matrix

Possible audiences:

- primary customer
- secondary customer
- buyer
- user
- partner
- distributor
- investor
- employee

Only include relevant groups.

---

# 68. Funnel / Journey Messaging

Where useful, map messages to:

```text
AWARENESS
CONSIDERATION
VALIDATION
PURCHASE
ONBOARDING
RETENTION
REFERRAL
```

---

# 69. Awareness Messaging

Usually emphasize:

- problem recognition
- relevance
- category understanding
- core value

---

# 70. Consideration Messaging

Usually emphasize:

- differentiation
- comparison
- proof
- fit

---

# 71. Purchase Messaging

Usually emphasize:

- confidence
- friction reduction
- offer clarity
- risk reduction
- CTA

---

# 72. Retention Messaging

Usually emphasize:

- value realization
- support
- progress
- relationship

---

# 73. Message Hierarchy Anti-Pattern

Avoid a hierarchy that is simply:

> Quality / Innovation / Service / Trust

without customer meaning or evidence.

---

# `Messaging Architecture`

Including:

- core message
- message hierarchy
- 3–5 message pillars
- supporting messages
- proof per pillar
- audience variants
- journey-stage variants

# STEP 4 — CORE MESSAGES, VALUE COMMUNICATION & PROOF LANGUAGE

# 75. Objective

Turn strategy into precise, credible customer-facing messages.

---

# 76. Problem Message

Define how the brand talks about the customer's problem.

Avoid:

- exaggerating fear
- insulting current choices
- manufacturing urgency

---

# 77. Desired Outcome Message

Define what better future state the brand helps create.

---

# 78. Value Proposition Message

Translate the strategic value proposition into natural customer language.

---

# 79. Differentiation Message

Explain the difference in terms of customer relevance.

Avoid:

> We are different because we care more.

unless care is operationally proven.

---

# 80. Brand Promise Message

Translate the promise into clear language that operations can support.

---

# 81. Proof Message

Every significant claim should connect to proof.

Possible proof forms:

- data
- process
- credentials
- guarantee
- customer evidence
- transparent comparison
- demonstration
- specification
- experience

---

# 82. Proof Language Rule

Proof should sound:

- specific
- calm
- transparent
- proportionate to evidence

Avoid inflated certainty.

---

# 83. Proof Claim Card

```yaml
claim:
customer_value:
proof:
proof_strength:
approved_wording:
prohibited_wording:
limitations:
```

---

# 84. Unsupported Superlative Rule

Words such as:

- best
- first
- only
- fastest
- safest
- number one

require evidence and, where relevant, legal review.

---

# 85. Guarantee Language

If guarantees exist:

- define exact scope
- define conditions
- do not imply broader protection than exists

---

# 86. Objection Messaging

Create principles for common objections.

Possible objections:

- price
- trust
- complexity
- switching
- time
- risk
- quality
- unfamiliarity

---

# 87. Objection Response Structure

```text
ACKNOWLEDGE
↓
CLARIFY
↓
EXPLAIN
↓
PROVE
↓
OFFER NEXT STEP
```

---

# 88. Comparison Messaging

If comparing against alternatives:

- be accurate
- be fair
- avoid misleading claims
- compare relevant dimensions

---

# 89. Price Messaging

Where relevant, define whether the brand explains price through:

- total value
- quality
- convenience
- risk reduction
- savings
- service
- expertise

Do not create fake justification.

---

# 90. Risk-Reversal Messaging

Possible forms:

- guarantee
- trial
- cancellation
- returns
- transparent process
- demo
- proof

Only use what actually exists.

---

# STEP 5 — BRAND STORY, ONE-LINER, ELEVATOR PITCH & CORE NARRATIVE

# 92. Objective

Create reusable narrative assets that explain the brand clearly without losing strategic focus.

---

# 93. One-Liner

The one-liner should explain in one concise statement:

```text
WHO WE HELP
+
WHAT WE HELP THEM ACHIEVE
+
WHY WE ARE DIFFERENT
```

Not every one-liner must include all components literally, but the logic should be present.

---

# 94. One-Liner Rule

Avoid vague statements such as:

> Empowering the future.

unless the context makes it meaningful.

---

# 95. Short Brand Description

Create a 2–4 sentence description suitable for:

- profile
- directory
- press intro
- partner introduction

---

# 96. Elevator Pitch

Create a practical 30–60 second explanation.

Suggested logic:

```text
CUSTOMER CONTEXT
↓
PROBLEM
↓
WHAT WE DO
↓
DIFFERENCE
↓
PROOF / VALUE
```

---

# 97. Elevator Pitch Rule

It should sound speakable.

Do not write a corporate paragraph that no human would say aloud.

---

# 98. Brand Story

The brand story should explain:

- why the brand exists
- what problem it responds to
- what it believes should be better
- how it approaches the problem differently
- what future it wants to help create

---

# 99. Brand Story Boundary

Do not invent founder history.

If origin facts are unavailable, do not fabricate them.

---

# 100. Origin Story

If a real founder origin exists, distinguish:

```text
FACTUAL_ORIGIN
STRATEGIC_INTERPRETATION
```

---

# 101. Customer-as-Hero Rule

Avoid making the company the only hero.

The story should often position the customer as the person achieving progress.

---

# 102. Brand-as-Guide Rule

If Phase 4 relationship role supports it, the brand story may place the brand as:

- guide
- partner
- enabler
- expert

rather than savior.

---

# 103. About Narrative

Create an About narrative that is more complete than the one-liner but still strategic.

---

# 104. About Narrative Structure

Possible structure:

```text
WHAT WE SEE
WHY IT MATTERS
WHAT WE DO
HOW WE ARE DIFFERENT
WHAT WE BELIEVE
WHAT CUSTOMERS CAN EXPECT
```

---

# 105. Mission/Vision Boundary

If mission and vision were not finalized earlier, Phase 5 may express customer-facing narrative versions only if strategically supported.

Do not create ceremonial corporate statements merely to fill sections.

---

# 106. Founder Voice Boundary

If the founder is part of the brand narrative, define when founder voice and brand voice differ.

---

# 107. Step 5 Output

Create:

# `Core Narrative Library`

Including:

- one-liner
- short description
- elevator pitch
- brand story
- About narrative
- origin narrative if factual
- founder-brand relationship note if relevant

All customer-facing text must be Persian.

# STEP 6 — VOCABULARY, WRITING RULES, CTA SYSTEM & CHANNEL ADAPTATION

# 108. Objective

Make the verbal identity usable by anyone writing for the brand.

---

# 109. Vocabulary System
