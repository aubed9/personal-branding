# 4. Skill Mission

The mission of this skill is to move the user from:

> “We know exactly what the brand means, how it sounds, and what messages it must communicate.”

to:

> “We have a strategically appropriate brand name direction, a rigorously evaluated shortlist or selected name, a clear tagline direction, and a creative concept system that tells the future visual identity team what the brand should express without prematurely designing the logo or visual system.”

Phase 6 turns verbal strategy into memorable brand assets and creative direction.

# 9. Naming Is Strategic, Not Decorative

A brand name is not merely:

> a word that sounds nice.

A useful name should support:

```text
POSITIONING
+
MEMORY
+
DISTINCTIVENESS
+
PRONUNCIATION
+
RELEVANCE
+
FLEXIBILITY
+
CULTURAL FIT
+
LEGAL FEASIBILITY
```

---

# 10. Naming Is Not an SEO Keyword Exercise

Do not prioritize literal keyword inclusion over:

- memorability
- distinctiveness
- brandability
- long-term flexibility

unless the business model specifically requires descriptive naming.

---

Founder preference matters.

But final name evaluation must separate:

```text

STRATEGIC_FIT
CUSTOMER_FIT
LINGUISTIC_FIT
COMMERCIAL_FEASIBILITY
LEGAL_RISK
```


# STEP 1 — NAMING STRATEGY & NAMING CRITERIA

# 35. Objective

Define how names will be generated and judged before brainstorming begins.

---

# 36. Naming Type Framework

Possible naming types:

```text
DESCRIPTIVE
SUGGESTIVE
EVOCATIVE
ABSTRACT
INVENTED
COMPOUND
METAPHORICAL
FOUNDER_BASED
GEOGRAPHIC
ACRONYM
FOREIGN_LANGUAGE
HYBRID
```

---

# 37. Descriptive Names

Strengths:

- category clarity
- easier immediate understanding

Risks:

- lower distinctiveness
- narrower expansion
- legal difficulty
- generic feel

---

# 38. Suggestive Names

Suggest a benefit, feeling, or idea without directly describing.

---

# 39. Evocative Names

Use association, metaphor, symbolism, or emotion.

---

# 40. Abstract Names

May be unrelated semantically but brandable through use.

Require more brand-building.

---

# 41. Invented Names

Can offer distinctiveness and legal opportunity.

Risks:

- pronunciation
- explanation
- artificiality

---

# 42. Compound Names

Combine meaningful words or fragments.

Check awkwardness and length.

---

# 43. Founder-Based Names

Can create trust but may reduce scalability or transferability.

---

# 44. Acronym Warning

Acronyms often have low memorability unless:

- naturally pronounceable
- already recognized
- meaningful in context

---

# 45. Naming Criteria Matrix

Evaluate names using:

```text
STRATEGY_FIT
TARGET_FIT
CATEGORY_FIT
DISTINCTIVENESS
MEMORABILITY
PRONUNCIATION
SPELLING
MEANING
EMOTIONAL_FIT
PERSONALITY_FIT
VOICE_FIT
PERSIAN_FIT
LATIN_FIT
CULTURAL_SAFETY
EXPANSION_FIT
DOMAIN_FEASIBILITY
LEGAL_FEASIBILITY
```

---

# 46. Mandatory vs Preferred Criteria

Separate:

```text
MUST_HAVE
SHOULD_HAVE
NICE_TO_HAVE
```

---

# 47. Knockout Criteria

Define immediate rejection rules.

Examples:

- offensive meaning
- severe pronunciation difficulty
- direct competitor conflict
- obvious legal conflict
- misleading category meaning
- unacceptable cultural association

---

# 48. Weighted Scoring

If useful, assign qualitative or numerical weights.

Do not hide subjectivity behind false precision.

---

# 49. Suggested Weight Logic

Higher weight often belongs to:

- strategic fit
- distinctiveness
- pronunciation
- cultural safety
- legal feasibility

Weights depend on project context.

---

# 50. Naming Risk Tolerance

Classify:

```text
CONSERVATIVE
BALANCED
BOLD
```

---

# 51. Name Length

Do not impose arbitrary character limits.

Evaluate:

- readability
- pronunciation
- digital use
- memory
- URL
- signage
- packaging

---

# 52. Sound / Phonetic Criteria

Consider:

- syllable count
- consonant difficulty
- vowel flow
- repetition
- rhythm
- ease of saying
- ease of hearing

---

# 53. Persian Phonetic Criteria

Check:

- natural Persian pronunciation
- difficult consonant clusters
- ambiguity in short vowels
- multiple likely pronunciations

---

# 54. Latin Transliteration Criteria

Check whether Persian names create:

- multiple spellings
- hard-to-type transliterations
- confusing letters
- inconsistent pronunciation

---

# 55. Semantic Criteria

Check:

- literal meaning
- implied meaning
- metaphor
- emotional association
- unintended meaning

---

# 56. Cultural Criteria

Check for:

- taboo
- offensive association
- political association
- religious sensitivity
- slang
- undesirable historical association

Do not rely on stereotypes.

---

# 57. Digital Criteria

Consider:

- searchability
- typing
- spelling recall
- social handles
- domain
- voice assistant pronunciation

---

# 58. Expansion Fit

A good name should not unnecessarily prevent:

- new category
- new city
- new country
- new product

if expansion is planned.

---

# `Naming Strategy & Evaluation Framework`

- rejected naming types
- must-have criteria
- should-have criteria

# 68. Metaphorical Territory

---

# 69. Origin Territory

# STEP 3 — NAME GENERATION, SCREENING & SHORTLISTING

# 77. Objective

Generate a broad candidate pool, then progressively screen until only strategically viable names remain.

---

# 78. Generation Rule

Do not evaluate too early during initial generation.

Use:

```text
DIVERGE
↓
SCREEN
↓
CONVERGE
```

---

# 79. Candidate Volume

Generate enough options to create real choice.

For a standard project, aim internally for:

```text
30–100+
```

raw candidates depending on complexity.

The final customer-facing document need not show every weak candidate.

---

# 80. Generation Methods

Possible methods:

- semantic association
- metaphor
- word combination
- morpheme combination
- word transformation
- clipping
- linguistic borrowing
- invented phonetics
- customer vocabulary
- category vocabulary
- origin references
- symbolic references

---

# 81. Persian Name Generation

When creating Persian names, consider:

- written form
- pronunciation
- rhythm
- semantic depth
- colloquial association
- classical / modern feel
- transliteration

---

# 82. English / Latin Name Generation

Consider:

- pronunciation by Persian speakers
- pronunciation internationally
- spelling
- accidental meanings
- transliteration back into Persian

---

# 83. Hybrid Name Generation

Hybrid Persian-English names require extra caution.

Check whether they feel:

- natural
- forced
- dated
- culturally coherent

---

# 84. Candidate Record

For each serious candidate:


# 85. Screening Stage 1 — Strategic Screen

Reject names that fail:

- positioning
- target
- brand character
- category fit

---

# 86. Screening Stage 2 — Linguistic Screen

Check:

- pronunciation
- spelling
- ambiguity
- awkwardness
- unintended word breaks

---

# 87. Screening Stage 3 — Semantic Screen

Check:

- literal meaning
- slang
- negative meaning
- competitor association

---

# 88. Screening Stage 4 — Cultural Screen

Check relevant markets and languages.

---

# 89. Screening Stage 5 — Digital Screen

Check:

- domain patterns
- search collisions
- social handle collisions
- typo risk

---

# 90. Screening Stage 6 — Preliminary Legal Screen

Check obvious trademark / company / competitor conflicts where data access exists.

This is not final legal clearance.

---

# 91. Homophone Risk

Check whether the name sounds like:

- inappropriate word
- competitor
- negative phrase
- confusing category term

---

# 92. Typo Risk

Evaluate common misspellings.

---

# 93. Search Collision

A name may be technically unique but impossible to search because of unrelated dominant meanings.

Mark as:

```text
SEARCH_COLLISION_RISK
```

---

# 94. Domain Strategy

Possible domain approaches:

```text
EXACT_MATCH
MODIFIED_DOMAIN
COUNTRY_TLD
CATEGORY_MODIFIER
BRAND_PLUS_ACTION
```

Do not reject a strong name solely because exact `.com` is unavailable unless exact-match domain is a critical requirement.

---

# 95. Domain Notes

For shortlisted names, capture:

```yaml

country_domain:
reasonable_alternatives:
search_collision:
risk:
```

---

# 96. Trademark Preliminary Screen

Capture:

```text
OBVIOUS_CONFLICT
POTENTIAL_CONFLICT
NO_OBVIOUS_CONFLICT_FOUND
NOT_CHECKED
```

Never write:

> Trademark is available.

unless legally confirmed through the appropriate authority.

---

# 97. Naming Scorecard

For shortlisted candidates, compare:

- strategy
- memorability
- pronunciation
- distinctiveness
- meaning
- personality
- Persian usability
- international usability
- expansion
- domain
- preliminary legal risk

---

# 98. Shortlist Size

Prefer:

```text
3 to 7
```

serious final candidates.

---

# 99. Shortlist Rule

Every shortlisted name must be defensible.

Do not include intentionally weak names merely to make one option look better.

---

# 100. Name Presentation

For each shortlisted name, show:

- name
- pronunciation
- meaning
- naming territory
- strategic rationale
- emotional effect
- strengths
- weaknesses
- domain notes
- legal notes
- risk
- confidence

---

# 101. Recommendation Rule

Recommend one name only if one candidate clearly has the strongest overall logic.

Otherwise present a ranked shortlist and define what test should decide.

---

# 102. Founder Preference Testing

If founder preference differs from strategic ranking:

- show the gap
- show risks
- allow explicit decision
- preserve the reasoning

---

# 103. Customer Naming Test

When necessary, test:

- pronunciation
- recall
- association
- category fit
- negative meaning
- comprehension

Do not ask only:

> Which name do you like?

---

# 104. Name Testing Warning

Consumer preference tests can over-favor familiar descriptive names.

Use tests as evidence, not absolute voting.

# STEP 4 — TAGLINE STRATEGY & TAGLINE DEVELOPMENT

# 106. Objective

Determine whether the brand needs a tagline and, if so, what strategic job it should perform.

---

# 107. Tagline Definition

A tagline is:

> a concise verbal asset that reinforces an important brand meaning.

It is not required for every brand.

---

# 108. Tagline Need Test

Ask:

- Does the name explain enough?
- Does category need clarification?
- Is positioning difficult to understand?
- Is emotional reinforcement valuable?
- Is a campaignable phrase strategically useful?

---

# 109. No-Tagline Option

`NO_TAGLINE` is a valid strategic decision.

Do not create one simply because branding documents usually include it.

---

# 110. Tagline Jobs

Possible jobs:

```text
CATEGORY_CLARITY
POSITIONING
PROMISE
BENEFIT
EMOTIONAL_MEANING
DIFFERENTIATION
MEMORY
CAMPAIGN_PLATFORM
```

Choose one primary job.

---

# 111. Tagline Territory

Develop 2–5 possible territories.

---

# 112. Tagline Criteria

Evaluate:

```text
STRATEGY_FIT
CLARITY
MEMORABILITY
DISTINCTIVENESS
VOICE_FIT
NAME_COMPATIBILITY
PROOFABILITY
LONGEVITY
TRANSLATABILITY
LEGAL_RISK
```

---

# 113. Tagline Length

Do not enforce arbitrary word count.

Prefer concise enough to remember and use.

---

# 114. Tagline Cliché Rule

Avoid phrases such as:

- quality you can trust
- your partner in success
- beyond expectations
- innovation for tomorrow

unless transformed into something specific and ownable.

---

# 115. Tagline Claim Rule

If tagline makes a factual claim, it must be provable.

---

# 116. Persian Tagline Rule

Natural Persian rhythm matters.

Do not translate English structures literally.

---

# 117. Bilingual Tagline Rule

If a tagline must work in Persian and English, evaluate each language independently.

Do not force literal equivalence.

---

# 118. Tagline Candidate Card

```yaml
tagline:
territory:
job:
meaning:
voice_fit:
name_fit:
strengths:
risks:
proof_need:
```

# 257. Definition of Skill Success

This skill succeeds when, at the end of Phase 6, a complete beginner:

1. Understands what the brand name needs to achieve.
2. Understands why naming starts with criteria instead of brainstorming.
3. Knows the appropriate naming styles.
4. Understands the naming territories.
5. Sees a curated serious candidate set.
6. Understands how candidates were screened.
7. Can pronounce shortlisted names.
8. Understands their meanings and associations.
9. Understands Persian and Latin spelling issues.
10. Understands cultural risks.
11. Understands domain risks.
12. Understands preliminary legal risks.
13. Knows that legal screening is not trademark clearance.
14. Has a selected name or an evidence-based final shortlist.
15. Understands whether a tagline is needed.
16. Understands what job the tagline performs.
17. Has a tagline shortlist or a justified no-tagline decision.
18. Understands the selected creative territory.
19. Understands what emotional impression the future visual identity should create.
20. Understands what visual clichés should be avoided.
21. Knows what the visual identity must feel like.
22. Knows what it must not feel like.
23. Understands unresolved risks and validation needs.
24. Has a complete Phase 7 Visual Identity Design Brief ready for execution.

# 258. Ultimate Phase 6 Rule

> **Phase 6 turns the brand's strategy, identity, and language into a memorable name system, a purposeful tagline decision, and a coherent creative direction — without prematurely designing the visual identity.**

Operational interpretation:

> Start from the locked Phase 5 Verbal Identity & Messaging System. Define naming criteria before generating names. Develop multiple naming territories. Generate a broad candidate pool. Screen candidates strategically, linguistically, semantically, culturally, digitally, and preliminarily legally. Produce a serious shortlist and select or recommend a name only when justified. Decide whether a tagline is needed and create one only if it performs a clear strategic job. Develop multiple creative territories and select a coherent creative direction that tells designers what the brand must mean and feel like without choosing final colors, fonts, or logo forms. Produce a complete Persian Naming, Tagline & Creative Direction Foundation and a Persian Phase 7 Visual Identity Design Brief.
